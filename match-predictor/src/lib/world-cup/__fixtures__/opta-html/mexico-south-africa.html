Three players were sent off as Mexico opened the 2026 World Cup with a 2-0 victory over nine-man South Africa.

Sphephelo Sithole and substitute Themba Zwane both saw red for South Africa, with Julián Quiñones and Raúl Jiménez on target for the co-hosts in Thursday’s Group A clash at Mexico City Stadium.

However, Williams played his part as Mexico netted the earliest goal in a World Cup opener since 2006.

Jiménez was thwarted again as Mexico pushed for a deserved second, before Quinones saw a low strike clip off the post.

Gilberto Mora‘s introduction from the bench sparked the crowd into life as he became the youngest player to feature for a host nation at the World Cup (17 years and 240 days), and Mexico soon had their second.

South Africa were the masters of their own downfall. Not only did they offer next to nothing going forward, having just three shots and creating chances worth only 0.07 xG, but they became the first team to receive two red cards in a World Cup match since Portugal and the Netherlands both had two players dismissed against each other in the 2006 round of 16.

  • Mexico remain unbeaten in their last eight FIFA World Cup openers (W6 D2) since losing 1-0 against Norway in their first game in 1994.
  • With South Africa’s Yaya Sithole and Themba Zwane, and Mexico’s César Montes all being sent off, this was the fifth FIFA World Cup match to see as many as three red cards, and first since Netherlands v Portugal in 2006 (4).
  • South Africa’s Yaya Sithole was the first player to be sent off in the opening match of a FIFA World Cup since Marco Etcheverry for Bolivia against Germany in 1994.
  • South Africa’s Themba Zwane became the second oldest player to be sent off in a FIFA World Cup match, behind Fernando Clavijo for USA against Brazil in 1994 (38y 162d).
  • South Africa were the first team to have two red cards in a FIFA World Cup match (Yaya Sithole and Themba Zwane) since both Netherlands and Portugal did so in their round of 16 match in 2006.
