Canada vs Bosnia-Herzegovina: Pre-Match Opta Facts

  • This will be the first ever encounter between Canada and Bosnia-Herzegovina.
  • The 2026 tournament will mark Canada’s third appearance at the FIFA World Cup, after 1986 and 2022 – as such, it’s the first time they have qualified for back-to-back tournaments.
  • Canada have lost all six of their FIFA World Cup matches, scoring just twice (including one own goal) and conceding 12 goals. Only El Salvador have played as many games at the tournament while losing them all.
  • This will be Canada’s sixth FIFA World Cup match against a team from the UEFA confederation, out of seven matches played in total.
  • The FIFA World Cup hosts have reached the second round of the competition in 20 of the previous 22 editions, with the exception being South Africa in 2010 and Qatar in 2022. The last hosts/co-hosts to win the tournament were France in 1998.
  • Canada have lost only one of their last 28 matches played in Toronto (W18 D9) – a 3-2 defeat against Jamaica in November 2023.
  • Bosnia-Herzegovina will appear at their second FIFA World Cup, after their maiden participation in Brazil 2014 (W1 L2) – they were eliminated at the group stage, despite winning their last game against Iran (3-1).
  • Bosnia-Herzegovina qualified via the UEFA play-offs, eliminating Wales and Italy on penalties. Overall, they lost only one of their 10 qualifying matches for the 2026 FIFA World Cup, a home defeat against Austria (1-2). They also found the net in all 10 of their matches.
  • Jonathan David has been directly involved in over a third of Canada’s goals across their last two major tournaments, with four goals and one assist in the 2024 Copa América and the 2025 CONCACAF Gold Cup.
  • Jesse Marsch will experience his first World Cup as head coach. It will be his third major tournament in three years with Canada, following a fourth-place finish at the 2024 Copa América and a quarter-final exit to Guatemala at the 2025 Gold Cup.
  • Edin Džeko was Bosnia-Herzegovina’s top scorer in qualifying for the 2026 FIFA World Cup, with six goals in nine matches. The 40-year-old played in all three of their games at their only previous World Cup in 2014, scoring in their 3-1 win over IR Iran.
  • Bosnia goalkeeper Nikola Vasilj played more minutes than any other individual in the UEFA qualifiers for the 2026 FIFA World Cup, completing the entirety of all 10 matches (960 minutes). He notably saved Wales’ final penalty in the play-off shootout.
