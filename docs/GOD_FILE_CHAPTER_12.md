# Chapter 12 – Match Prediction Models

# Part I – Prediction Framework

## 12.1 Purpose

### 12.1.1 Objective

The Match Prediction Models transform the expected goals estimates produced by the Expected Goals Engine into probabilistic forecasts of football match outcomes.  
Whereas Chapter 11 estimated the expected attacking performance of the Home and Away teams, the purpose of this chapter is to describe how those expectations are converted into probabilities for the range of possible match results.  
The Match Prediction Models therefore represent the first probabilistic stage of the Graham League Prediction Model (GLPM), converting deterministic expected goals estimates into statistical forecasts suitable for decision-making and league simulation.

### 12.1.2 Scope

The Match Prediction Models receive the following inputs from the Expected Goals Engine:

* Home Expected Goals (xGH)   
* Away Expected Goals (xGA) 

Using these estimates, the models compute probability distributions describing possible match outcomes.  
Typical outputs include:

* Home Win Probability   
* Draw Probability   
* Away Win Probability   
* Correct Score Probabilities   
* Goal Total Probabilities   
* Both Teams to Score Probability   
* Team Goal Distributions 

These outputs provide the quantitative foundation for the league simulations described in the following chapter.

The Match Prediction Models occupy the stage immediately following the Expected Goals Engine.

# 12.2 Prediction Philosophy

## 12.2.1 Probabilistic Forecasting

Football matches are inherently uncertain.  
Even when two teams possess identical expected goals estimates, numerous scorelines remain possible due to the stochastic nature of goal scoring.  
Accordingly, the GLPM predicts probabilities rather than single deterministic outcomes.  
Instead of predicting that a team *will* win, the framework estimates the likelihood of every plausible result.  
This probabilistic approach more accurately reflects the uncertainty associated with football matches and provides richer information for downstream analysis.

## 12.2.2 Expected Goals as Predictive Inputs

The Match Prediction Models do not operate directly on the GLPM Rating Vectors.  
Instead, they use the calibrated Home and Away Expected Goals generated by the Expected Goals Engine.  
This design preserves the modular architecture established throughout the GLPM framework by separating:

* team ability estimation;   
* expected performance estimation; and   
* probabilistic outcome prediction. 

## 12.2.3 Separation of Responsibilities

The GLPM prediction architecture consists of three complementary layers.

| Component | Responsibility |
| ----- | ----- |
| Rating Framework | Estimate latent football ability |
| Expected Goals Engine | Estimate expected goals |
| Match Prediction Models | Estimate probabilities of football outcomes |

Each component performs a distinct function while passing standardized outputs to the next computational stage.

# Part III – Match Outcome Prediction

# 12.8 Match Result Probabilities

## 12.8.1 Purpose

The primary objective of the Match Prediction Models is to estimate the probability of each possible match outcome.  
Using the Goal Probability Matrix produced by the Dixon–Coles model, individual scoreline probabilities are aggregated into the three mutually exclusive match results:

* Home Win   
* Draw   
* Away Win 

These probabilities provide the principal forecasts generated by the Graham League Prediction Model.

## 12.8.2 Home Win Probability

The Home Win Probability is obtained by summing the probabilities of all scorelines in which the Home Team scores more goals than the Away Team.  
Mathematically,  
PHome Win\=i\>jPj  
where Pjrepresents the probability of the Home Team scoring igoals and the Away Team scoring jgoals.

## 12.8.3 Draw Probability

The Draw Probability is obtained by summing the probabilities of all scorelines in which both teams score the same number of goals.  
PDraw\=iPi  
This includes outcomes such as:

* 0–0   
* 1–1   
* 2–2   
* 3–3 

and any higher-scoring draws included within the probability matrix.

## 12.8.4 Away Win Probability

The Away Win Probability is calculated as  
PAway Win\=i\<jPj  
representing all scorelines where the Away Team scores more goals than the Home Team.

## 12.8.5 Probability Consistency

The three match outcome probabilities satisfy  
PHome Win\+PDraw\+PAway Win\=1.  
This ensures that the predicted outcomes form a complete probability distribution.

# 12.9 Correct Score Predictions

## 12.9.1 Purpose

Beyond predicting the overall match result, the Match Prediction Models estimate the probability of every feasible scoreline.  
Each scoreline corresponds to one element of the Goal Probability Matrix generated by the Dixon–Coles model.

## 12.9.2 Scoreline Ranking

Correct score predictions are produced by ranking scorelines according to their estimated probabilities.  
For example,

| Rank | Scoreline | Probability |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 1 | 1–0 | Highest |
| 2 | 1–1 | Second Highest |
| 3 | 2–1 | Third Highest |

The highest-ranked scoreline represents the most likely individual result but should always be interpreted within the broader probability distribution.

## 12.9.3 Interpretation

Correct score probabilities should not be interpreted as deterministic predictions.  
Even the highest-probability scoreline typically represents only a modest proportion of the total probability mass, reflecting the inherent uncertainty of football outcomes.

# Part IV – Advanced Prediction Enhancements

# 12.12 Model Calibration

## 12.12.1 Purpose

Accurate probability estimation requires that predicted probabilities correspond closely to observed match outcomes over time.  
The Match Prediction Models therefore incorporate calibration procedures to improve the statistical reliability of their forecasts.  
Calibration seeks to minimise systematic bias while preserving the relative ordering of predicted probabilities.

## 12.12.2 Calibration Objectives

The calibration process aims to:

* improve probability accuracy;   
* reduce systematic forecasting bias;   
* maintain consistency across competitions;   
* improve long-term predictive reliability. 

These objectives support the production of well-calibrated probability estimates suitable for both match prediction and league simulation.

# Part V – Integration with the GLPM Framework

# 12.16 Integration with the Expected Goals Engine

## 12.16.1 Overview

The Match Prediction Models operate directly upon the expected goals estimates produced by the Expected Goals Engine described in Chapter 11\.  
This separation of responsibilities ensures that the statistical estimation of expected goals remains independent from the probabilistic estimation of football outcomes.  
The Expected Goals Engine provides the offensive expectations for both competing teams, while the Match Prediction Models transform those expectations into complete probability distributions describing every feasible match outcome.

## 12.16.2 Computational Interface

The interface between the Expected Goals Engine and the Match Prediction Models is intentionally simple and standardized.  
For every fixture, the prediction models receive:

* Home Expected Goals (xGH)   
* Away Expected Goals (xGA) 

These values represent the expected attacking output of each team after accounting for their GLPM Rating Vectors, contextual match variables, and the interactions modelled by the Expected Goals Engine.  
The Match Prediction Models then use these expected goals as the principal parameters for the Dixon–Coles probability model.

## 12.16.3 Separation of Computational Layers

The computational workflow is organised into distinct modelling layers.

| Layer | Function |
| ----- | ----- |
| GLPM Rating Framework | Estimate latent football abilities |
| Expected Goals Engine | Estimate expected goals |
| Match Prediction Models | Estimate outcome probabilities |
| League Simulation Models | Simulate complete competitions |

Each layer performs a clearly defined task before passing standardized outputs to the next stage of the framework.  
This modular architecture improves maintainability, interpretability, and flexibility while reducing unnecessary dependencies between modelling components.

# 12.17 Integration with League Simulation Models

## 12.17.1 Purpose

Individual match predictions represent only one level of the overall prediction framework.  
The probabilities generated by the Match Prediction Models are subsequently supplied to the League Simulation Models, where they are used to estimate season-long outcomes across complete competitions.  
Thus, match prediction serves as the foundation upon which league forecasting is constructed.

## 12.17.2 Simulation Inputs

For every scheduled fixture, the League Simulation Models receive the probabilistic outputs generated in this chapter, including:

* Home Win Probability;   
* Draw Probability;   
* Away Win Probability;   
* Correct Score Distribution;   
* Goal Distributions;   
* Additional derived prediction markets where applicable. 

These probabilities are used to simulate the outcome of individual fixtures repeatedly throughout the season.

## 12.17.3 Season Forecasting

By repeatedly simulating every scheduled fixture, the League Simulation Models estimate:

* Expected league positions;   
* Expected points totals;   
* Championship probabilities;   
* Qualification probabilities;   
* Relegation probabilities;   
* Team finishing distributions;   
* Other season-level forecasting metrics. 

Repeated simulation enables the framework to quantify uncertainty throughout an entire league season rather than considering fixtures independently.

# 12.19 Design Principles

## 12.19.1 Transparency

Every stage of the prediction framework has been designed to remain interpretable.  
Intermediate outputs—including Rating Vectors, expected goals, and scoreline probabilities—can be independently examined and validated before contributing to subsequent prediction stages.  
This transparency facilitates model evaluation and supports future methodological improvements.

## 12.19.2 Modularity

Each component within the framework performs a single, clearly defined responsibility.  
As a consequence:

* improvements to the Rating Framework do not require modification of the Expected Goals Engine;   
* improvements to the Expected Goals Engine do not require modification of the Match Prediction Models;   
* improvements to the Match Prediction Models do not require modification of the League Simulation Models. 

This modular structure promotes long-term maintainability and simplifies future development.

## 12.19.3 Extensibility

The prediction framework has been designed to accommodate future methodological developments.  
Potential enhancements include:

* improved statistical models;   
* AI-assisted parameter optimisation;   
* ensemble prediction methods;   
* competition-specific calibration;   
* dynamic model updating;   
* real-time prediction adjustments. 

These enhancements can be incorporated while preserving compatibility with the overall GLPM architecture.
