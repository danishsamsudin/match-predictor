Oh Hyeon-Gyu and Hwang In-Beom scored as an impressive South Korea team came from behind to beat Czechia 2-1 in their opening World Cup match at Estadio Guadalajara.

Group A’s second game was a clash of styles as Czechia survived wave after wave of South Korea pressure before striking from a long throw in the 59th minute, via Ladislav Krejci’s towering header.

And, after the offside flag thwarted Tomas Soucek, Hwang teed up the winner, with substitute Oh ensuring South Korea joined Mexico on three points at the top of Group A.

South Korea looked far more composed in possession throughout a first half in which Czechia did not attempt a shot on target, with Lee Kang-In working Matej Kovar from distance in the 14th minute.

South Korea continued to force the issue after the interval, with Kovar blocking Hwang’s low drive and Lee Jae-Sung’s follow-up, then producing his best save yet to thwart Son following more intricate approach play from Lee Kang-In.

Czechia’s set-piece prowess then came to the fore, though, with Wolves defender Krejci steaming towards the six-yard box to nod Vladimir Coufal’s throw past the stranded Kim Seung-gyu. 

  • South Korea have lost only one of their last seven opening matches at the FIFA World Cup dating back to 2002 (W4 D2), a 1-0 defeat to Sweden to in 2018.
  • South Korea have come back to win two of the last three FIFA World Cup matches in which they conceded first (L1), having done so only twice in the first 27 matches in which they conceded the first goal (D6 L19) including none of the previous 10.
  • This is South Korea’s eighth all-time win at the FIFA World Cup and the fourth by a 2-1 scoreline. They conceded the opening goal in all four of those matches.
  • Czechia have lost all four of their FIFA World Cup matches in Guadalajara (3 in 1970 as Czechoslovakia, 1 today), becoming the second team to lose four FIFA World Cup matches in a single city (Belgium in Mexico City – also 4).
  • Including Ladislav Krejcí’s goal in this match, seven of Czechia’s last 14 goals in the FIFA World Cup dating back to 1990 have been headers (including as Czechoslovakia).
