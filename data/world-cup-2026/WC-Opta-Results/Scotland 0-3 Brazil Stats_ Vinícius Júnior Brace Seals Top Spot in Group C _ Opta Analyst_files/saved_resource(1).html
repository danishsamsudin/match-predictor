MATCH PREVIEW

Scotland play Brazil for a FIFA World Cup group stage match at Miami Stadium on Wednesday with the Brazilian side looking to clinch a place in the Round of 32.

Scotland's last group stage match saw them beaten for the first time in the competition this season 1-0 by Morocco on June 19. In six games this year, they have three wins and three losses.

Brazil won for the first time in the competition this year in their last group stage game on June 19, beating Haiti 3-0 at Philadelphia Stadium, improving their unbeaten streak to five. Matheus Cunha led the team with a brace. In 2026, they have won four, drawn one, and lost one.

Brazil are atop the group on four points, while Scotland's three points place them third. Brazil are currently in line for the Round of 32, while Scotland are out of the knockout round places but still on for a place in the round of 32.

Morocco are second in the group on four points, well-positioned to advance to the Round of 32. Haiti are in danger of elimination, sitting fourth on zero points.

In the last encounter between the sides in March 2011, it was Brazil who won 2-0.

Scotland have been led by John McGinn in World Cup play. He is their only scorer with one goal. Brazil's leading players going forward have been Vinícius Júnior and Cunha, who have each scored twice in this World Cup campaign. They are tied for fourth in the competition's scoring charts.

Brazil have scored in 10 straight contests across all competitions. They haven't been shut out since a World Cup Qualifiers meeting with Bolivia in September 2025.

In their last six matches in all competitions, Scotland have won three and lost three. The Scottish side scored nine and conceded four over that period, scoring first in two of the six contests. A majority of their goals (six) came in the first half, while their opponents netted three times in the first 45 minutes. The Brazilian side have won four, drawn one and lost one in their last six. The Brazilian side scored 16 and conceded seven over that stretch, netting first in four of the six. They had eight goals in the first half, while their opponents netted four times before the break.
